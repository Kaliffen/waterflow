Goal 2026-08-29-a1d8 goes from thirty-two records without a `kind` to ten, and
the ten that remain cannot be reached by consolidation at all: every one belongs
to a subject that never had an item.

Measured after running the fold on this repository's five finished subjects.
Nineteen files moved to history -- eighteen superseded and one watermark -- and
no superseded record is left in the retrieval path.

**The ceiling is structural.** Consolidation fires on a subject that had items
and has none open. A subject settled entirely in `align` -- `writer`, `unborn`,
`rule-homes`, `proof`, `goal`, `consolidate`, `agent-lifecycle`,
`critique-axes`, `waterflow-store`, `field-proof` -- never had an item, so it
never finishes, so its records keep no `kind` for as long as the store exists.
Ten of them, and they are disproportionately the `idiom` records: the decisions
and terms, which are the records most likely to be read years later and least
likely to be re-derived.

This is not an argument for consolidating untracked subjects. The trigger is
sound precisely because it does not guess: an empty frontier means "never
tracked" as often as "finished", which is why that check exists. It is an
argument that the goal as written cannot be met by the mechanism it names, for
the second time -- the first was fixed by the extension in store-ontology-6b9e,
and this is the residue.

Two ways out, neither taken here. Either an `align` that settles a subject
publishes an item for it, so decisions become trackable work and the trigger
reaches them, or a kind is written at emission from the beginning and the whole
question retires as the old records age out. The second is what actually
happens if nothing is done, which is the argument for doing nothing.
