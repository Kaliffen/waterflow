A goal is a measurable target that gates a decision, and it never closes; an
item is work that does.

Settled by the owner. The two look alike and are not, and conflating them has
already cost something. waterflowtest tracked "the whole thing holds its frame
budget at 50k agents" as item crowd-0009: opened, closed, then reopened when it
was measured at forty seconds rather than fifteen. Closing it meant "measured
once, liked the answer". A target is met or unmet, and saying so is a different
operation from finishing a task.

A goal names the threshold **and the measurement that decides it**. That is the
part items cannot carry and the part that was missing when waterflowtest2
reported a mean inside budget while its own worst case was outside: nothing had
ever said which statistic the goal was about.

It also gives a gated decision a home. The claim "no GPU backend is needed yet"
currently sits inside a `prove` record's gist, attached to a number, where
nothing can find it. As a goal it is what the number is for.

A goal with no observation against it is **unmeasured**, and `recall` reports it
as such. Without that a goal is an aspiration, which is how every target system
decays.
