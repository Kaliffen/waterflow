At most one writer touches a working tree at a time, session or agent, and
ownership friction over a single value is read as a seam problem rather than a
coordination problem.

Supersedes 2026-08-28-4c39, whose every claim still holds and whose scope was too
narrow. Fan-out branches stay separate, an overlap is corroboration reported
once, a contradiction between branches is escalated rather than averaged, and
slice must draw the blocking edge or merge when two slices would touch the same
paths.

What changed is the word "agent". The rule bound agents, and the collision that
prompted it was between two peer sessions in one checkout: the crowd-sim store's
b6e0 records a rendering constant living in the simulation config, edited twice
from one side while two sessions negotiated over the number across four messages.
Nothing in the rule was ever agent-specific, so it extends to any concurrent
writer for free.

The second half is the part with teeth. That defect broke no build and failed no
assertion -- a constant in the wrong project cannot. What surfaced it was two
parties editing the same value, and the seam atom now reads that friction as
evidence about ownership rather than as a scheduling problem to be fixed with
more coordination. It catches a class of defect no assertion can, which is why
it is worth a rule of its own.
