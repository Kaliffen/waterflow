At most one agent writes to the working tree at a time; parallel implementation
requires separate trees and disjoint scope, and failing either one serializes.

The owner reported two distinct collisions, and they take two fixes. Agent
results collide because fan-out branches were never told how to reconcile:
branches now stay separate, an overlap is corroboration reported once, and a
contradiction between branches is escalated as a finding rather than averaged.
Working trees collide because nothing forbade concurrent writers: two agents
editing the same tree is a merge conflict arranged in advance, and neither can
prove anything because the ground moves under both.

Consequence for `slice`: two open unblocked slices are a promise they can run at
once, so slices that would touch the same paths must either carry a blocking
edge or be merged.
