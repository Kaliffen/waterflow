A skill stating what to *do* at the point of doing it is not a second home for
the rule that makes it necessary: `slice` keeps "either draw the blocking edge or
merge them" while topology.md keeps disjoint scope as a condition.

Settled by the owner after both critique axes independently flagged the pair as
a surviving duplication. The distinction is between a rule and an action taken
under it. topology.md says why two writers cannot share a tree; `slice` says
what to do about it at the one moment a slicer can act, which is while drawing
edges. Stripping the sentence would leave `slice` pointing at a reference for
the instruction that is arguably its whole job at that step.

This is the boundary Authoring Rule 5 needs and did not have. "No rule lives in
two places" was being read as "no rule is mentioned twice", which cannot be
satisfied by any skill that has to act on a rule. The test is whether removing
the text loses an instruction or only loses a restatement.
