The store stops being one flat kind of record and becomes five, marked by a
`kind:` field: fact, observation, idiom, goal, watermark.

Settled by the owner. The problem is contamination, not volume: a trace and a
truth come back from `recall` looking identical, and the older one wins by
sounding certain.

- **fact** -- known because we made it, checked it, or proved it.
- **observation** -- seen in a signal and believed, not verified. A debt: it is
  promoted to a fact by running something that could have failed.
- **idiom** -- a philosophy that drives decisions, permanent unless revisited,
  tagged with the domain concepts it governs.
- **goal** -- a measurable target that gates a decision. Never closes.
- **watermark** -- the process summary, linking the others.

**Why it is not a taxonomy tax.** Every existing atom sorts into it without
forcing: `seam` and a pass/fail `prove` are facts, `critique` and `dig` are
observations, `define` and `interrogate` are idioms, `slice` is a watermark.
`prove` splits on a line the repository already owns -- proof has to be able to
fail -- so a measurement is an observation and a verdict is a fact. Nobody has
to deliberate.

**The evidence it is real.** waterflowtest's a882 reported peak pressure as 35k
from a single sample four seconds into a run, and it stood until someone
measured properly and found 438k. Nothing in a flat store could have flagged it,
because there was no way to say "this is what I saw, not what I checked".

The field cannot be called `tier`; that is the model dial. `idiom` was chosen
over `principle` and over `sediment`, the last because this repository's own
prose already uses sediment to name the failure the store exists to prevent.
