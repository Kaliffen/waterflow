When a subject has no open items left, `land` consolidates: watermarks move to
`.waterflow/history/`, and what they established survives as facts,
observations, idioms and goals.

Settled by the owner across a full round.

**Trigger.** After `close_item`, `land` checks the state surface. No open items
for the subject means the work said it was done, so a nine-slice build
consolidates once at the end and an active subject is never touched. Consolidating
per land would rewrite the same knowledge on every slice.

**Disposal.** Superseding hides a record from `recall`, which honours the
supersedes list, but leaves the file in the retrieval directory where a raw grep
or a careless brief still finds it. Moving it to `.waterflow/history/` makes the
exclusion structural rather than conventional: nothing globs that path, the file
survives, and `git blame` is intact. "Read the store" then returns only what is
currently believed.

**What survives.** Not a summary of the work -- the work is what is being thrown
away. What survives is what is true: the facts, the observations still owed a
check, the idioms, and the goals. This is a fold, not an archive. Processes are
the events, the knowledge is the projection.

**Why no drift.** Nothing changes unless a process runs, so a fact cannot rot on
its own; the next process to touch that code updates the facts when it
consolidates its own watermarks. The convention is not enforced, and when drift
is observed anyway the answer is to synchronise with reality -- inspect, or
`dig` -- rather than to block a commit or to trust the record.

Nothing new is invented to do this. Supersession already evicts, `list_frontier`
already answers whether work is open, and `land` already writes to the state
surface at integration.
