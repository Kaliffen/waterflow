The build writes the address and the fold empties the body, and that split is
also the whole of migration: a store converts as fast as its subjects are
touched, and there is no migration pass.

**The actor was forced, not chosen.** At emission the declaration usually does
not exist yet, so there is nothing to address. The address can only be written at
the moment the comment is, which is the build. A build that grafts adds `#Symbol`
to the `scope` entry of the record it grafted from and does nothing else to it.

**An existing store needs nothing done to it.** No record written before grafting
carries a `#`, so nothing detects one as grafted, the fold's step finds none, and
both strips are no-ops on a path without one. Such a record keeps its full
expression and goes stale in the ordinary way when the code moves, which is the
right outcome and not a debt.

**A migration pass is forbidden rather than merely unnecessary.** Sweeping a
store for old duplicates is precisely the tidying chore the contract says any
step asking for is wrong, and the judgement of what to keep would be a person's
rather than the kinds'.

**Without naming the actor, an old duplicate could never convert**, because the
`#` that finds it is only ever written at emission. Naming the build as the
writer makes old and new records migrate by the same rule, with nothing added
for the old case. Found by asking what a repository already carrying a store does
on upgrade — before the field test rather than during it. Extends
[[2026-08-30-1f4e]] rather than overturning it.
