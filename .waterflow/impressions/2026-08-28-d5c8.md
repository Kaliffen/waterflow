A prove record may carry an optional `conditions:` field naming the parameters a
measurement depends on; staleness stays revision-drift and the gate is not
touched.

The failure this answers is in the crowd-sim store. A performance number taken
fifteen seconds into a scenario with a rising cost curve was fresh, passing and
false, three times running -- see that store's e21d and item crowd-0009. Revision
drift cannot catch it, because the code did not move. The conditions did.

Owner rejected leaving it to discipline: it had already been left to discipline
and it failed three times. Owner also rejected the stronger form, where a proof
reporting a measurement must name its conditions and is `blocked` without them.
That reading is the honest one but it puts a judgement call -- is this result a
measurement? -- inside the gate, and a gate deciding that will be wrong in both
directions, refusing ordinary suites and waving through bare numbers.

The field is therefore a place for the fact to exist rather than a rule that
enforces it. What it buys is that `recall` surfaces "15s run-in" beside the
number, which is the entire failure: nobody was lying, the phase was simply not
written down anywhere the next reader would look.
